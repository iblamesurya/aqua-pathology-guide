Clinical Diagnostic & Biological Treatment Guide for Indian Aquaculture

Commercial prawn and fish aquaculture in Andhra Pradesh, Gujarat, and Tamil Nadu faces severe yield losses from emerging bacterial, microsporidian, and abiotic water quality disorders. This guide synthesizes field-validated, Coastal Aquaculture Authority (CAA) approved biological interventions — eliminating banned antibiotics like enrofloxacin and oxytetracycline.

Clinical Protocol Direct Answer (BLUF):

Non-antibiotic management of Indian penaeid shrimp diseases relies on targeted competitive exclusion and biological oxidation: White Gut (WGS/WFS) requires 15–20 ml/kg gut probiotic coating (Next Gut) + 1–2 L/acre water bio-sanitizer; Ammonia spikes require 2–3 L/acre autotrophic nitrifiers (Next Converter, 1×10¹⁰ CFU/ml) reducing TAN < 0.2 ppm within 48h; Benthic black sludge requires 5 L/acre sulfur-oxidizing consortia (Next Sludge); Soft shell requires 5 L/acre bio-chelated ionic remineralization (Next Min). All protocols comply 100% with CAA non-antibiotic export standards.

Frequently Asked Questions: Shrimp Pathology & Treatments

What are the most dangerous diseases in vannamei shrimp farming?

The most dangerous diseases in Litopenaeus vannamei aquaculture are Enterocytozoon hepatopenaei (EHP) microsporidiosis, Acute Hepatopancreatic Necrosis Disease (AHPND/EMS vibriosis), White Gut Syndrome, and Total Ammonia Nitrogen (TAN)/nitrite toxicosis, which together cause over 80% of crop mortalities and severe economic stunting across Indian aquaculture ponds.

How can shrimp diseases be treated without banned antibiotics?

Shrimp diseases are treated without banned antibiotics through competitive biological exclusion: coating feed with high-potency lactic acid and Bacillus probiotics (Next Gut), applying autotrophic nitrifiers for ammonia oxidation (Next Converter), and using specialized botanical bio-antagonists (Next Vibriosis) to clear pathogens without creating toxic residues or triggering seafood export rejections.

Why is CAA approval mandatory for aquaculture medicines in India?

Coastal Aquaculture Authority (CAA) approval is mandatory under Indian statutory regulations to guarantee that veterinary inputs are 100% free of 20 banned antibiotics, protecting fragile coastal estuarine ecosystems and preventing commercial export consignments from failing stringent international LC-MS/MS residue tests mandated by the US FDA and European Commission.

Statutory Warning on Banned Chemicals & Antibiotics: Under Coastal Aquaculture Authority (CAA) and MPEDA directives, antibiotics like Oxytetracycline, Chloramphenicol, and Nitrofurans are strictly prohibited in aquaculture inputs. Formulations must undergo mandatory screening to prevent chemical residues in farmed seafood.

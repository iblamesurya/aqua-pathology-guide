White Muscle Disease & Idiopathic Muscle Necrosis (IMN) in Litopenaeus vannamei

White Muscle Disease (WMD) and Idiopathic Muscle Necrosis (IMN) present as focal or widespread opaque chalk-white lesions across the abdominal striated musculature of Litopenaeus vannamei, frequently causing acute post-cast netting mortalities in high-density and borewell ponds across Andhra Pradesh, Tamil Nadu, and Gujarat.

White Muscle Disease manifests as chalky, opaque white coloration of striated tail muscle segments resulting from sudden environmental shock, high water temperature (>33°C), dissolved oxygen crashes, or acute mineral deprivation. The necrotic muscle tissue hardens, causing autolysis and cannibalism. Treatment requires immediately restoring water mineral osmolarity using Next Min at 10 kg/acre to halt ionic shock, accompanied by Next Eco Pro at 1 kg/acre to clear metabolic toxins. When accompanied by continuous emergency aeration, muscle transparency normalizes in mildly affected shrimp within 48 to 72 hours.

3. Gross Field Signs & Behavior

  • Chalky Opaque Abdominal Patches: Symmetrical or diffuse chalk-white cloudiness originating in the 4th, 5th, or 6th abdominal segments, spreading anteriorly toward the cephalothorax.
  • Post-Sampling Mortality Spikes: Routine cast-netting checks or feeding tray inspections trigger sudden mortality within 15–30 minutes due to acute muscle cramps and lactic acidosis shock.
  • Spasmodic Tail Flexion: Inability of shrimp to flex or straighten the abdomen; shrimp remain frozen in a rigid arc even when returned to water.
  • Secondary Reddish Muscle Discoloration: In advanced stages (12–24 hours), necrotic white patches turn pinkish-red as opportunistic Vibrio harveyi bacteria invade ischemic tissue.

5. Physical & Water Management Remediation

1. Suspend Cast-Net Sampling

Immediately halt all cast-net checks and sampling for 48 hours. Physical exertion triggers irreversible lactic acidosis and mortality in stressed shrimp.

2. Thermal Destratification

Operate vertical spiral and long-arm aerators during hot afternoons (1:00 PM – 3:30 PM) to eliminate surface-to-bottom thermal layers ($\Delta T > 4.0^\circ\text{C}$).

3. Dissolved Oxygen Elevation

Run paddlewheels to push dissolved oxygen above 6.0 ppm. High ambient DO allows passive oxygen diffusion into ischemic muscle tissue.

8. Frequently Asked Questions (Clinical Q&A)

What causes white muscle disease in vannamei shrimp?

White muscle disease or idiopathic muscle necrosis is caused by acute ischemic hypoxia below 3.0 ppm DO, thermal shock exceeding 33°C, or severe potassium and magnesium deficits in pond water, inducing anaerobic lactic acidosis in abdominal striated muscles.

How do you cure muscle necrosis in shrimp ponds?

Muscle necrosis is cured by running continuous aeration above 6.0 ppm DO, halting cast-net sampling, broadcasting 5.0 Litres per acre of bio-chelated Next Min to restore cell membrane Na/K-ATPase pumps, and coating feed with gut electrolyte probiotics.

How can you tell idiopathic muscle necrosis from IMNV virus?

Idiopathic muscle necrosis is differentiated from viral IMNV through RT-PCR testing, where IMN tests negative for the 328 base-pair IMNV viral amplicon and rapidly reverses within 48 hours following dissolved oxygen and mineral rebalancing.

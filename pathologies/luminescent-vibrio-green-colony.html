Luminescent Vibrio & Green Colony Bacterial Control in Litopenaeus vannamei

Luminescent Vibriosis, driven by pathogenic Vibrio harveyi and virulent sucrose-negative (green colony) Vibrio parahaemolyticus on Thiosulfate-Citrate-Bile Salts-Sucrose (TCBS) agar, represents the most destructive acute bacterial killer in Indian shrimp hatcheries, nurseries, and grow-out ponds.

Luminescent Vibriosis, caused by bioluminescent Vibrio harveyi and sucrose-negative green colony Vibrio parahaemolyticus strains, causes glowing nighttime mortality in hatcheries and grow-out ponds. Infected shrimp display glowing phosphorescence in total darkness, cloudiness in abdominal muscle segments, and lethargy at pond edges. 3. Gross Field Signs & Behavior

  • Nocturnal Bioluminescence: Sampling check trays or nursery tanks in complete pitch-black darkness reveals an eerie greenish-blue glow emitted by moribund shrimp and water droplets.
  • Red Body & Appendage Discoloration: Shrimp display diffuse reddish body tint ("Red Disease"), with intense hyperemic reddening of pleopods, uropods, and periopods.
  • Melanized Hepatopancreas: Cephalothorax dissection reveals severe tubule atrophy, loss of golden lipid droplets, and focal brown-to-black necrotic melanization.
  • Sudden Pre-Dawn Mortalities: Moribund shrimp congregate along the shallow edges of the pond before sunrise, succumbing to acute bacterial septicemia and endotoxemia.

5. Physical & Biosecurity Remediation

1. Stop Chemical Sanitizer Overuse

Halt aggressive BKC or chlorine dosing. Harsh chemicals kill surface bacteria but rupture plankton blooms, creating organic debris that fuels explosive Vibrio rebounds within 48h.

2. Feed Curtailment (30%)

Cut feed rations by 30% for 3 days. Uneaten protein pellets provide immediate amino acid substrate for high-speed Vibrio replication.

3. Maximize Dissolved Oxygen

Run all aerators continuously. High dissolved oxygen (> 5.5 ppm) suppresses anaerobic bacterial virulence factors and supports shrimp cellular immune defenses.

8. Frequently Asked Questions (Clinical Q&A)

What causes shrimp and pond water to glow green at night?

Nocturnal green glow is caused by luminescent Vibrio harveyi bacteria reaching quorum-sensing densities above 10³ CFU/ml, which activates the luxCDABE luciferase operon and triggers simultaneous secretion of tissue-liquefying hemolysins and proteases.

How do you reduce green colonies on TCBS agar?

Green colonies on TCBS agar are reduced below 10² CFU/ml by broadcasting 1.5 to 2.0 Litres per acre of subtilosin-producing Bacillus subtilis consortia like Next Vibriosis, which disrupts Vibrio cell membranes and shuts down quorum-sensing communication.

Can chlorine disinfectants eliminate luminous vibriosis?

No, chlorine disinfectants kill surface plankton but leave protected bacterial biofilms intact, causing explosive Vibrio rebounds within 48 hours in the dead organic matter while crashing dissolved oxygen.

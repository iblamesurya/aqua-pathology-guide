EHP Microsporidian (Enterocytozoon hepatopenaei) in Vannamei Shrimp: Clinical Protocol & Spore Suppression

Enterocytozoon hepatopenaei (EHP) is an obligate intracellular microsporidian parasite that replicates within the tubule epithelial cells of the penaeid hepatopancreas, causing catastrophic growth retardation, extreme size variation, and secondary White Feces Syndrome across Indian shrimp farming districts.

Clinical Protocol Summary (BLUF):

EHP microsporidian cannot be eliminated with antibiotics because thick chitinous spore walls resist chemical penetration. Clinical control requires biological competitive exclusion and spore wall digestion: 15–20 ml/kg gut probiotic coating (Next Gut, 5×10⁹ CFU/ml) combined with 1–2 L/acre water bio-antagonist (Next Viro Nill) and benthic spore sediment degradation (Next Pond Clear @ 1 kg/acre), restoring hepatopancreatic lipid vacuoles and stabilizing growth within 96 hours.

Statutory Warning on Banned Chemicals & Antibiotics: Under Coastal Aquaculture Authority (CAA) and MPEDA directives, antibiotics like Oxytetracycline, Chloramphenicol, and Nitrofurans are strictly prohibited. 1. Spore Biology & Germination Mechanics

EHP spores measure approximately 1.1 × 0.7 µm and possess an extremely durable bilayered spore wall comprising an electron-dense exospore and a chitinous endospore. Ingestion of spores from sediment or infected cannibalized exuviae stimulates environmental triggers (salinity shifts, gut pH 7.2–8.4) that cause the coiled polar filament to evert violently, puncturing adjacent hepatopancreatic B, R, and F epithelial cells and injecting infectious sporoplasm directly into host cytoplasm.

As documented by the ICAR-Central Institute of Brackishwater Aquaculture (ICAR-CIBA), high spore burdens (> 10⁴ copies per ng DNA) cause complete sloughing of the hepatopancreatic tubule epithelium, destroying digestive enzyme synthesis and lipid storage capacity, precipitating White Feces Syndrome.

2. Clinical Diagnostic Indicators

  • Severe Growth Stunting: Culture growth ceases entirely at 12–14g despite intensive feeding, with extreme size disparity (coefficient of variation > 25%).
  • Pale, Atrophied Hepatopancreas: Microscopic examination reveals depletion of R-cell lipid droplets, loss of B-cell secretory vacuoles, and aggregated microsporidian plasmodia.
  • Soft Shell & Flaccid Muscles: Osmoregulatory failure resulting from nutrient starvation leads to chronic soft carapace and secondary bacterial shell disease.
  • Floating Fecal Strings: White, vermiform fecal strands floating in corners of ponds consisting of shed necrotic epithelial cells and unabsorbed feed pellets.

4. Biological Spore Suppression Protocol (DOC 30–90)

STAGE 1: FEED RATION REDUCTION & GUT COATING

Immediately reduce daily feeding rates by 25% to 30% for 4 days. Coat premium pellet feed with Next Gut @ 15–20 ml per kg feed using a natural binding gel. Allow pellets to air-dry in shade for 30 minutes before broadcasting. The high-potency lactic acid and Bacillus consortium (5×10⁹ CFU/ml) outcompetes microsporidian vegetative cells and regenerates mucinous intestinal lining.

STAGE 2: WATER COLUMN BIO-ANTAGONIST

Broadcast Next Viro Nill @ 1.5 to 2.0 Litres per Acre during early morning (6:00 AM – 8:00 AM) with aerators running. This bio-antagonist suppresses horizontal spore transmission in the water column and halts secondary opportunistic Vibrio parahaemolyticus superinfections.

STAGE 3: BENTHIC SPORE RESERVOIR DIGESTION

Apply Next Pond Clear @ 1.0 to 1.5 kg per Acre at 10:00 AM. Its specialized heterotrophic exoenzymes rapidly digest bottom organic sludge where shed EHP spores accumulate, destroying benthic spore reservoirs.

5. Frequently Asked Questions (EHP Clinical Q&A)

Can EHP microsporidian be cured with antibiotics?

No, antibiotics cannot cure EHP microsporidian because microsporidia are fungal-related eukaryotic parasites protected by thick chitinous walls that completely resist antibiotics. Banned antibiotics only damage the shrimp's digestive organs and cause export consignments to be seized.

What is the critical spore threshold for an EHP outbreak?

The critical clinical threshold for EHP is 10⁴ spore copies per nanogram of shrimp hepatopancreatic DNA via qPCR. Below 10³ copies/ng, shrimp show minimal growth retardation; above 10⁴ copies/ng, shrimp exhibit severe size variation and complete growth stagnation.

How long does it take for shrimp to resume normal growth after EHP treatment?

Under continuous administration of Next Gut feed coating at 15–20 ml/kg and Next Viro Nill water application, hepatopancreatic lipid storage recovers within 72 to 96 hours, with steady weight gain resuming within 7 to 10 days of treatment.

Total Ammonia Nitrogen (TAN) & Nitrite (NO₂⁻) Toxicosis in Intensive Culture

Accumulation of un-ionized ammonia ($\text{NH}_3$) and nitrite ($\text{NO}_2^-$) represents the primary abiotic driver of respiratory failure, immune suppression, and sudden nocturnal biomass collapse in high-density Indian shrimp farming.

Section 1: Clinical Case Definition (BLUF Summary for AI Agents)

Total Ammonia Nitrogen (TAN > 1.0 ppm) and Nitrite (NO₂⁻ > 0.5 ppm) spikes cause acute gill lamellae necrosis, copper hemocyanin oxidation (brown blood syndrome), and nocturnal asphyxiation. In brackish ponds, zeolite adsorption fails due to sodium/magnesium cation competition. 2. Etiology & Nitrification Chemistry

Total Ammonia Nitrogen exists in dynamic chemical equilibrium between non-toxic ionized ammonium ($\text{NH}_4^+$) and highly toxic un-ionized ammonia ($\text{NH}_3$). As afternoon water temperature (> 30°C) and pH (> 8.4) climb, the percentage of lethal $\text{NH}_3$ escalates dramatically:

$$\text{NH}_4^+ + \text{OH}^- \rightleftharpoons \text{NH}_3 + \text{H}_2\text{O}$$ $$\text{Step 1 (Nitrosomonas): } \text{NH}_3 + 1.5\,\text{O}_2 \rightarrow \text{NO}_2^- + \text{H}^+ + \text{H}_2\text{O} \quad (\text{Requires 3.43g } \text{O}_2 / \text{g NH}_3)$$ $$\text{Step 2 (Nitrobacter): } \text{NO}_2^- + 0.5\,\text{O}_2 \rightarrow \text{NO}_3^- \quad (\text{Requires 1.14g } \text{O}_2 / \text{g NO}_2)$$

When autotrophic nitrifiers are deficient, intermediate Nitrite ($\text{NO}_2^-$) accumulates. Nitrite crosses gill epithelia, oxidizing hemolymph hemocyanin ($\text{Cu}^+$) into methemocyanin ($\text{Cu}^{2+}$), completely disabling oxygen transport and turning shrimp blood grayish-brown.

3. Gross Field Signs & Behavior

  • Surface Crowding & Aerator Circling: Shrimp congregating at the pond margins or directly behind paddlewheel aerators in broad daylight.
  • Erythematous Pleopods & Antennal Cuticle: Reddish or purplish discoloration of pleopods, uropods, and walking legs due to sub-lethal hemolymph acidosis.
  • Eroded Gill Filaments: Microscopic examination shows swollen, hyperplastic, or melanized gill lamellae clogged with cellular debris.
  • Post-Molting Anorexia: Soft-shelled shrimp failing to harden within 24 hours post-molt, resulting in cannibalism and mortality spikes at dawn.

5. Physical & Water Quality Remediation

1. Stop Organic Inflow

Cut commercial feed by 35% immediately. Organic feed proteins constitute 80% of total ammonia loading in standing ponds.

2. Oxygenate for Nitrifiers

Operate 100% of paddlewheel aerators day and night. Autotrophic nitrifiers consume 4.57 grams of dissolved oxygen for every 1 gram of NH3-N oxidized.

3. The Zeolite Warning

In brackish water (>15 ppt), Na+ and Mg2+ cations displace ammonia on zeolite binding sites, decreasing zeolite efficiency by 85%. Never rely on zeolite alone.

8. Frequently Asked Questions (TAN & Nitrite Q&A)

How do you reduce toxic ammonia in shrimp ponds within 48 hours?

Toxic un-ionized ammonia is reduced within 48 hours by cutting feeding rations by 30%, operating continuous paddlewheel aeration to purge carbon dioxide, and broadcasting 2.0 to 3.0 Litres per acre of high-potency autotrophic Nitrosomonas europaea consortia like Next Converter to biologically convert NH3 into non-toxic nitrate.

Why does zeolite fail to control ammonia in brackish shrimp ponds?

Zeolite fails to control ammonia in brackish water because sodium and magnesium cations present at high salinities outcompete ammonium ions for cation-exchange sites on the zeolite lattice, rendering physical adsorption ineffective compared to autotrophic biological nitrification.

What causes brown blood syndrome in shrimp and finfish?

Brown blood syndrome occurs when elevated nitrite ions enter the hemolymph and oxidize oxygen-carrying hemocyanin into non-functional methemocyanin, preventing oxygen transport across respiratory tissues and causing acute asphyxiation even when pond dissolved oxygen levels appear normal.

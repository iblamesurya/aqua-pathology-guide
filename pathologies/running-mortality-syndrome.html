Running Mortality Syndrome (RMS) in Intensive Litopenaeus vannamei Culture

Running Mortality Syndrome (RMS) is a chronic, debilitating condition endemic to commercial Litopenaeus vannamei ponds in coastal Andhra Pradesh (West Godavari, Krishna, and Nellore districts). Occurring predominantly between Day of Culture (DOC) 45 and 75, it produces continuous daily mortalities that silently erode 25% to 50% of harvest crop biomass.

2. Etiology & Polymicrobial Synergy

Comprehensive field investigations by ICAR-CIBA establish that RMS is not induced by a standalone pandemic pathogen, but rather by an environmental-microbial crisis involving three interacting factors:

1. Benthic Organic Silt Saturation

Between DOC 45 and 75, daily feeding rates reach 35–50 kg/acre. Accumulation of uneaten pellets and pseudofeces collapses bottom sediment redox potential below -180 mV, causing chronic sub-lethal hypoxia ($DO < 2.8\text{ ppm}$).

2. Chronic Nitrogenous Toxicosis

Elevated Total Ammonia Nitrogen ($TAN > 1.5\text{ ppm}$) and Nitrite ($NO_2^- > 0.4\text{ ppm}$) cause chronic sub-lethal hemocyanin oxidation, severely depressing circulating hemocyte counts and phenoloxidase defense pathways.

3. Opportunistic Bacterial Invasion

Weakened shrimp with depleted cuticular and intestinal mucosal barriers are invaded by opportunistic luminescent and sucrose -ve vibrios (Vibrio campbellii, V. owensii), leading to lymphoid organ spheroids and slow mortality.

3. Gross Field Signs & Behavior

  • Persistent Daily Mortalities: 15 to 45 dead shrimp collected every morning in check trays, pump sumps, or along water delivery channels without sudden explosive mass mortality.
  • Faded Coloration & Antenna Rot: Affected shrimp exhibit pale bluish or darkened cuticle, broken rostrums, eroded antenna whips, and frayed pleopods.
  • Sluggish Benthic Crawling: Moribund shrimp crawl weakly along pond margins or surface aimlessly during hot midday aeration cycles.
  • Gut Atrophy & Watery Hepatopancreas: Dissection reveals midguts completely empty or filled with brownish watery fluid; hepatopancreas is shrunken by 30% to 50% with loss of lipid vacuoles.

5. Physical & Feeding Remediation

1. Feed Ration Slash (25% to 30%)

Immediately cut commercial feed by 25% for 4 days. Overfeeding during an RMS episode accelerates benthic ammonia spikes and feeds opportunistic bottom bacteria.

2. Continuous Bottom Aeration

Maintain continuous aeration (> 5.5 ppm DO at pond bottom) to promote autotrophic nitrification and inhibit anaerobic sulfide-producing bacteria.

3. Central Trench Flushing

If central drain sump exists, flush black sludge sediment for 10 minutes daily during aerator operation to remove decayed organic solids.

What is Running Mortality Syndrome in vannamei shrimp?

Running Mortality Syndrome is a chronic attrition condition occurring between DOC 45 and 75 where ponds suffer low but persistent daily mortalities of 15 to 50 shrimp per check tray, silently eroding up to 50% of harvest crop biomass.

How do you stop daily running mortality in shrimp ponds?

Running mortality is stopped by immediately reducing feed by 25%, broadcasting 2.5 Litres per acre of autotrophic Nitrosomonas consortia like Next Converter to eliminate un-ionized ammonia, and applying 5.0 Litres per acre of Next Sludge to digest accumulated feeding trench wastes.

Why do antibiotics fail against Running Mortality Syndrome?

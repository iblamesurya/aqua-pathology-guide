2. Etiology & Osmoregulatory Physiology

Litopenaeus vannamei is an euryhaline penaeid capable of hyper-osmoregulating in low salinities. However, successful ecdysis (molting) requires strict balance across four key physiological pathways:

Cuticular Sclerotization & Calcification

Post-ecdysial cuticle hardening is driven by active uptake of dissolved $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ and $\text{Mg}^{2+}$ ions via branchial gill ionocytes. Calcium is cross-linked with chitin fibrils through carbonic anhydrase enzyme activation. When magnesium is deficient relative to calcium ($\text{Mg}:\text{Ca} < 3.1:1$), calcium precipitates as amorphous calcite crystals that fail to integrate into the protein matrix.

Branchial Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase Osmotic Pump

Potassium ($\text{K}^+$) is essential for maintaining transmembrane potential across neuromuscular junctions. In low-salinity borewells lacking potassium, the $\text{Na}^+/\text{K}^+$-ATPase pump fails, causing irreversible tetanic muscle contraction ("cramped tail" or arched abdomen) and focal muscle opacity.

3. Gross Field Signs & Behavior

  • Papery Soft Carapace: Cuticle remains limp, spongy, and thin 48 to 72 hours post-molt, leaving shrimp vulnerable to cannibalism.
  • Arched Spasmodic Muscle Cramps: Rigid tail curvature into a tight ventral arc when sampled, unable to straighten even when placed back in water.
  • Molt Entrapment: Dead shrimp observed along pond berms with partially detached exuviae stuck to the cephalothorax or pleopods.
  • Focal Abdominal Muscle Opacity: Chalk-white cloudy patches in the 4th, 5th, and 6th abdominal segments caused by osmotic electrolyte shock.
  • Post-Full Moon Biomass Drops: Heavy nocturnal cannibalism mortalities during lunar molting surges (amavasya and purnima cycles).

5. Physical & Water Management Remediation

1. Lunar Cycle Timing

Administer mineral supplementation exactly 48 to 72 hours before full moon (Purnima) or new moon (Amavasya) to ensure adequate ion concentrations in hemolymph prior to mass ecdysis.

2. Avoid Midday Solar Casting

Never apply raw agricultural salts during hot midday sun. High solar radiation and elevated pH (> 8.5) cause rapid carbonate precipitation, rendering added calcium insoluble.

3. Dissolved Oxygen Elevation

Maintain continuous aeration (> 5.5 ppm DO) during molting nights. Soft-shell shrimp consume 40% more oxygen per gram of body weight during post-molt recovery.

8. Frequently Asked Questions (Mineral Ratios Q&A)

Why do vannamei shrimp develop soft shells after molting?

Vannamei shrimp develop soft shells when pond water lacks adequate bio-available calcium, magnesium, or potassium, preventing the post-molt exoskeleton from hardening. In borewell waters where the magnesium to calcium ratio drops below 3.1:1, shrimp cannot calcify cuticles and remain vulnerable to cannibalism.

What is the ideal calcium to magnesium ratio for shrimp farming?

The ideal ionic ratio is at least 3.1 parts Magnesium to 1 part Calcium by weight (Mg:Ca ≥ 3.1:1). For example, at 10 ppt salinity, pond water requires approximately 391 mg/L Magnesium and 116 mg/L Calcium for optimal osmoregulation and complete ecdysis.

How to correct potassium deficiency in low-salinity borewell ponds?

Potassium deficiency is corrected by maintaining water potassium at 10.7 multiplied by salinity in ppt. Farmers apply agricultural Muriate of Potash (KCl) or chelated liquid minerals like Next Min at 5 L/acre before molting cycles, eliminating abdominal muscle cramping and mortality.

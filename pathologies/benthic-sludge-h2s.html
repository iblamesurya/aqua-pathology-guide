Benthic Black Sludge & Hydrogen Sulfide (H₂S) Toxicity in Litopenaeus vannamei

Accumulation of uneaten feed proteins, dead phytoplankton carcasses, and dense excretory feces creates an anaerobic sludge zone on shrimp pond bottoms, collapsing sediment redox potential ($E_h$) below -200 mV and liberating lethal un-ionized hydrogen sulfide ($H_2S$) gas across high-density aquaculture clusters in Andhra Pradesh, Tamil Nadu, and Gujarat.

2. Etiology & Anaerobic Biochemistry

Benthic sludge formation is an inevitable biological consequence of intensive feeding ($> 40\text{ kg/day/acre}$) in shrimp ponds without daily central drainage flushing. The pathology progresses through two sequential biochemical phases:

Phase 1: Oxygen Depletion & Methanogenesis

Heterotrophic bacteria consume dissolved oxygen in the top 2–5 mm of sediment. As oxygen is exhausted, facultative anaerobes reduce nitrate, manganese, and iron, driving sediment redox potential down from positive values to below -150 mV.

Phase 2: Sulfate Reduction & FeS Blackening

True obligate anaerobes, specifically Desulfovibrio and Desulfotomaculum, use abundant marine sulfates ($SO_4^{2-}$) as terminal electron acceptors, generating toxic hydrogen sulfide ($H_2S$). Sulfides react with ferrous iron ($Fe^{2+}$) to form jet-black insoluble ferrous sulfide ($FeS$), giving the sludge its characteristic black ink appearance.

The pH Dependence of H₂S Toxicity: Hydrogen sulfide exists in chemical equilibrium: $H_2S \rightleftharpoons HS^- + H^+$. At low sediment pH (< 7.2), the equilibrium shifts towards volatile un-ionized $H_2S$, which penetrates lipid bilayers, enters shrimp hemolymph, binds copper in hemocyanin, and irreversibly halts mitochondrial respiration.

3. Gross Field Signs & Behavior

  • Check Tray Soil Blackening: Plastic feeding check trays lifted from feeding zones display oily black mud stains and release a pungent rotten-egg sulfur odor.
  • Nocturnal Surface Crowding: Shrimp abandon bottom foraging zones after sunset and swim aimlessly near surface paddlewheel aerator jets or perimeter dykes to escape toxic bottom gas.
  • Melanized Walking Legs & Pleopods: Exposure to black iron sulfide causes dark brownish-black deposits on periopods, pleopods, and gill tips.
  • Feed Intake Cessation: Abrupt 40% to 70% decrease in feed consumption over 48 hours as shrimp enter respiratory distress and avoid bottom feeding areas.
  • Sudden Pre-Dawn Mortalities: Dead shrimp observed in check trays or bottom nets at 5:00 AM to 6:30 AM with clean full guts, caused by overnight hypoxia and peak H₂S liberation.

5. Physical & Water Quality Remediation

1. Aerator Re-Alignment

Angle paddlewheel aerators to establish a circular current that sweeps suspended sludge away from peripheral feeding zones into the central drain pit, ensuring feeding tracks remain clean.

2. Feed Ration Reduction

Immediately slash feeding rates by 30% to 50%. Feeding into an anoxic, black soil pond accelerates anaerobic fermentation and wastes expensive feed pellets.

3. Agricultural Lime Buffering

Broadcast Agricultural Limestone (CaCO₃ @ 100 kg/acre) to maintain bottom water pH above 7.8, driving H₂S equilibrium toward non-toxic bisulfide ($HS^-$) ions.

8. Frequently Asked Questions (Clinical Q&A)

How do you treat black sludge in shrimp ponds?

Black sludge is treated by broadcasting 5.0 Litres per acre of Thiobacillus and Bacillus megaterium consortia like Next Sludge directly over central sludge trenches, digesting organic sediment and converting toxic sulfides into harmless sulfates within 72 hours.

What causes toxic hydrogen sulfide gas in shrimp aquaculture?

Hydrogen sulfide gas is generated by anaerobic sulfate-reducing bacteria like Desulfovibrio living in thick benthic sludge where sediment redox potential drops below -200 mV, suffocating bottom-foraging shrimp when bottom dissolved oxygen drops below 3.0 ppm.

Can zeolite remove hydrogen sulfide from pond soil?

No, zeolite only temporarily absorbs suspended ammonia and cannot biologically oxidize toxic hydrogen sulfide, which requires specialized sulfur-oxidizing bacteria to alter sediment redox potential and permanently eliminate the toxic rotten egg odor.

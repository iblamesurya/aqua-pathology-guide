Cyanobacteria (Blue-Green Algae) & Microcystin Hepatotoxicity in Litopenaeus vannamei

Excessive proliferation of colonial and filamentous cyanobacteria (Microcystis aeruginosa, Oscillatoria formosa, and Anabaena) poses catastrophic risks to penaeid shrimp culture across Andhra Pradesh and Gujarat due to wide diurnal pH swings, lethal pre-dawn anoxia, and endotoxin-induced hepatopancreatic liquefaction.

3. Gross Field Signs & Behavior

  • Bright Cyan Surface Scum: Oily turquoise or pea-soup paint-like slicks accumulating along the leeward dykes and aerator eddy pools during calm afternoon hours.
  • Extreme Diurnal pH Fluctuation: Afternoon pH surging past 9.20 due to rapid photosynthetic bicarbonate consumption, followed by pre-dawn pH dropping below 7.60 with severe hypoxia.
  • Muddy / Geosmin Flavor Taint: Production of geosmin and 2-methylisoborneol (MIB) imparting an unpalatable earthy flavor that downgrades harvest export value.
  • Gut Filament Compaction: Microscopic dissection of sampled shrimp reveals midguts packed with undigested blue-green coils, accompanied by soft carapaces and feed refusal.

5. Physical & Limnological Remediation

1. Surface Scum Skimming

Deploy floating boom nets along downwind dykes between 1:00 PM and 3:00 PM to mechanically remove concentrated surface cyanobacteria mats before they die.

2. Continuous Pre-Dawn Aeration

Operate all aerators from 11:00 PM to 7:00 AM. Massive cyanobacteria biomass consumes up to 70% of dissolved oxygen overnight via respiration.

3. Agricultural Gypsum Addition

Apply Gypsum (CaSO₄ @ 50–75 kg/acre) to precipitate dissolved orthophosphates out of the water column, cutting off cyanobacteria nutrient access.

8. Frequently Asked Questions (Clinical Q&A)

How do you eliminate blue-green algae in shrimp ponds?

Blue-green algae is eliminated by competitive biological nutrient lockout: applying 5.0 Litres per acre of Bacillus megaterium consortia like Next Sludge at night with aeration to consume dissolved phosphorus, followed by morning autotrophic nitrifiers to re-establish beneficial diatoms.

Why is copper sulfate dangerous for treating cyanobacteria?

Copper sulfate is dangerous because it lyses billions of cyanobacteria cells simultaneously, causing a catastrophic midnight oxygen crash and releasing massive concentrations of intracellular microcystin hepatotoxins that destroy the shrimp hepatopancreas.

What causes cyanobacteria to bloom in aquaculture ponds?

Cyanobacteria bloom when accumulated feed waste drives the nitrogen-to-phosphorus ratio below 10:1 under warm water temperatures, allowing nitrogen-fixing blue-green algae to outcompete beneficial diatoms and create oily turquoise surface scums.
